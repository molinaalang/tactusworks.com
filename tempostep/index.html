Keep your hands on the instrument.

TempoStep is an iPhone metronome that listens. When you stop playing, it moves the tempo toward your target.

What a session looks like.

  1. 1

    Play

    Set a starting tempo and press start. Stay quiet for two seconds while it measures the room. Then play.

  2. 2

    Pause

    Stop. Shake out your hand, look at the fingering, stretch. It hears the quiet and waits.

  3. 3

    Step

    Start playing again and the new tempo is already running. The screen flashes to another color for a second when it steps.

  4. 4

    Target

    Reach your target and you get a chime, three taps, and a color flash. The screen says "target reached."

Rest between passes as long as you like.

Shake out your hand, look at the fingering again, work out what went wrong. Some metronome apps keep climbing on a timer while you do. TempoStep waits, and when you start playing, the next tempo is already set.

Four ways to reach the target.

  • Step up

    The default. Each stop adds your step until you reach the target.

  • Shuffle

    A random tempo near the target on every stop. How Shuffle helps.

  • Spiral

    Climbs three steps, drops back part of the way, then climbs on.

  • Step down

    Starts fast and works down to the floor you set.

Shuffle: a different tempo every pass.

Shuffle picks a random tempo near your target each time you stop, within 10, 20 or 30 percent. Use it on passages you can already play at tempo, so they don't settle at one speed. Full details in the manual.

  • It sticks. Harder in the session, better retained the next day.
  • You stay listening. If you can't predict the tempo, you can't coast. You hear mistakes instead of drilling them in.
  • Any tempo works. On stage the tempo is rarely the one you practiced. Practicing around a target gets you ready for that.

Based on research on interleaved practice in music (Carter & Grahn, 2016; Mathias & Goldman, 2025).

Also in the app.

  • Microphone calibration

    Each time you press start, it spends two seconds measuring your room and sets the level your playing has to cross.

  • Runs in the background

    Auto advance keeps working while you use forScore, IMSLP, a tuner, or anything else.

  • Tap tempo

    Tap along a few times and the tempo follows. Use it to match a recording or your teacher.

  • Subdivisions

    None, 8ths, triplets or 16ths. Set in Settings.

  • Hold tempo

    The lock keeps the tempo where it is while it keeps listening. Auto mode only.

  • Manual mode

    Listening off, no calibration. Press next for the next tempo, back to return.

  • Metres

    Tap the time signature to pick 2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 5/4, 6/8, 7/8, 9/8 or 12/8. In 6/8, 9/8 and 12/8 the accent falls on every third beat.

  • Colors

    Yellow, green, coral, sky, lilac, paper or ink.

  • Two clicks

    Woodblock and tactus.

  • Quiet screen

    Controls fade after three seconds of playing. Tap to bring them back. Hold your finger on any control to see what it does.

Questions.

Does it record me?

No. The mic only checks whether you are playing. Audio is processed on your device as it arrives and is never recorded, saved or sent. With listening off (Manual), the mic isn't used.

Will it confuse my playing with other sounds?

Each time you press start, TempoStep measures the room for two seconds and sets a threshold above that level. Steady noise such as HVAC or traffic stays below it. Your instrument goes above it. If it misses your stops or reacts to noise, change listening sensitivity in Settings.

What if I want to hold the tempo longer?

Tap the lock. It keeps listening but the tempo stays put. Unlock and it carries on from there.

When should I use Shuffle instead of Step Up?

Step Up climbs toward a target. Shuffle is for passages you already play at tempo. Random tempos stop you memorizing one speed. The manual covers all four modes.

What if a microphone isn't practical?

Tap the ear icon to switch to Manual. The mic turns off, calibration is skipped, and you press next when you want the tempo to move. Modes, subdivisions and metres still work. The hold lock does not.

Is there a subscription?

No. You pay $4.99 once and there are no ads.

Android?

Not yet. An Android version is planned; there is no date.
